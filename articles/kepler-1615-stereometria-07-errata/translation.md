# Errata

::: {.editorial-note}
English translation of the 1615 Linz printing, made for Plain Old Text. The errata correct words of the Latin text, so the words to be found and the corrections are left in Latin; only Kepler's instructions are translated. References are to signatures and leaves: "D. 2, verso" is the back of the second leaf of gathering D.
:::

Page D. 2, verso: *for greater clearness read thus:* *menta ista segmenti, quorum basis est semi circulus sint alt. s. p. & s. 1. s. in o. a. r. p. eandem ad cylindri segmentum æquealtum, quæ est totius vel primi segmenti ad totum æquealtum Cylindrum, sc. 7 ad 33. Idem enim locum habet etiam in segmentis segmenti quorum basis est circulus, semper enim segmentum hac basi, sive altū sive humile, est Cylindri sui æquealti dimidium. Quibus verò bases non s. c. e. s. s. a. c. segmenta, illis bases itidem istæ singulæ singulas conciliant proportiones. Itaque etiam de h. c. s. s. v. a. n. m. q. d. c. e. p. q. q insistant eidem segmento circuli, s. u. a: quæ verò eidem lineæ segmenti circularis, sint ut seg. altitudinis respondentia.* (That is: these segments of a segment whose base is a semicircle are, at every height, in the same ratio to the cylindrical segment of equal height as the whole or first segment is to the whole cylinder of equal height, namely 7 to 33; for the same holds in segments of a segment whose base is a circle, since a segment on this base, high or low, is always half its cylinder of equal height; and where the bases are not semicircles but other segments of a circle, those bases bring in their own ratios. So of these too it holds that those standing on the same segment of a circle are as their heights, and those on the same line of a circular segment correspond to the heights.)
