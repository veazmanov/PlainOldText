## []{.page n=129}Discourse on the Cause of Gravity.

To find an intelligible cause of gravity, we must see how it can come about, supposing in nature only bodies made of one and the same matter, in which we consider no quality and no inclination to approach one another, but only different sizes, shapes and motions; how, I say, it can come about that many of these bodies nevertheless tend directly towards one and the same centre, and keep gathered round it; which is the most common and the chief phenomenon of what we call gravity.

The simplicity of the principles I admit leaves little choice in this inquiry. For one judges at once that there is no likelihood of attributing to the shape, or to the smallness, of corpuscles any effect like that of gravity; which, being an effort, or an inclination to motion, must in all likelihood be produced by a motion. So that it remains only to seek in what manner it can act, and in what bodies it can be found.

[]{.page n=130}Looking simply at bodies, without that quality called gravity, their motion is naturally either straight or circular: the first belonging to them when they move without hindrance, the other when they are held round some centre, or turn on their own centre. We know to some extent the nature of straight motion, and the laws bodies keep in communicating their motions when they meet. But so long as we consider only this kind of motion, and the reflections that arise from it among the parts of matter, we find nothing that determines them to tend towards a centre. We must therefore come of necessity to the properties of circular motion, and see whether there is any among them that can serve us.

I know that Mr. Descartes too tried in his Physics to explain gravity by the motion of a certain matter turning round the Earth; and it is much to have been the first to have this thought. But it will be seen, from the remarks I shall make in the course of this discourse, in what his way differs from the one I am about to propose, and also in what it has seemed to me defective.
