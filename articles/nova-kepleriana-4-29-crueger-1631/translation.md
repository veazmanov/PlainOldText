# Crüger to Müller, Easter Monday 1631

::: {.editorial-note}
English translation of the Latin extracts printed in *Nova Kepleriana* 4 (1927), made for Plain Old Text. Each paragraph translates the corresponding extract of the original; the omission marks (…) are the 1927 editors'.
:::

… I shall follow the thread of your letter, which begins with the death of Kepler, a man for all astronomers to look up to. The first I heard of it was a vague rumour, not then worth believing. Then came letters to me from Lauban in Silesia, and even then I did not quite believe it. But now that yours have brought the same news, my hands have, as it were, become eyewitnesses. Truly we have reason to grieve from the heart, for his incomparable genius and for the works he left unfinished and had promised the public, above all the *Hipparchus*, the Foundations of the Rudolphine Tables, and Brahe's observations. For there is much in the Rudolphine Tables whose computation I find easy to grasp, but whose reasoning is not clear to me. …

So that I grieve most deeply that the man was taken from us before he had finished the works we so longed for. As for your thinking that I have a store of the Muses at hand to adorn the dead man's tomb, I am sorry to say you are mistaken …
