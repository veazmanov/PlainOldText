# General Scholium

English translation of the 1713 Cambridge printing, made for Plain Old Text. Each paragraph translates the corresponding paragraph of the original. Newton's "duplicate" and "sesquiplicate" ratios are given as the squares and the 3/2 powers of the distances. In the second footnote his "Act. 7. 27, 28" is given as Acts 17:27–28, where Paul quotes Aratus, and "Reg. 8. 27" as 1 Kings 8:27.

[]{.page n=481}The hypothesis of vortices is beset with many difficulties. For each planet to describe, by a radius drawn to the Sun, areas proportional to the times, the periodic times of the parts of the vortex would have to be as the squares of their distances from the Sun. For the periodic times of the planets to be as the 3/2 powers of their distances from the Sun, the periodic times of the parts of the vortex would have to be in that same proportion of the distances. For the smaller vortices around Saturn, Jupiter and the other planets to keep whirling and to float undisturbed in the Sun's vortex, the periodic times of the parts of the Sun's vortex would have to be equal. The rotations of the Sun and the planets about their axes disagree with all these proportions. The motions of comets are perfectly regular, and keep the same laws as the motions of the planets, and cannot be explained by vortices. Comets are carried with very eccentric motions into all parts of the heavens, which cannot happen unless vortices are done away with.

Projectiles, in our air, feel only the resistance of the air. Take away the air, as is done in Boyle's vacuum, and the resistance ceases, since a light feather and solid gold fall with equal speed in this []{.page n=482}vacuum. And the case is the same for the celestial spaces above the Earth's atmosphere. All bodies must move most freely in those spaces; and so the planets and comets must revolve perpetually in orbits given in kind and position, according to the laws set out above. They will indeed persist in their orbits by the laws of gravity, but they could by no means have first acquired the regular position of their orbits by those laws.

The six principal planets revolve about the Sun in circles concentric with the Sun, with the same direction of motion, very nearly in the same plane. Ten moons revolve about the Earth, Jupiter and Saturn in concentric circles, with the same direction of motion, very nearly in the planes of the planets' orbits. And all these regular motions do not have their origin in mechanical causes, since comets are carried freely in very eccentric orbits, and into all parts of the heavens. By this kind of motion the comets pass through the orbits of the planets very swiftly and very easily, and in their aphelia, where they are slower and linger longer, they are as far from one another as possible, and attract one another as little as possible. This most elegant system of the Sun, planets and comets could have arisen only from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful Being. And if the fixed stars are the centres of similar systems, all these, being built by a like counsel, will be subject to the dominion of *One*: especially since the light of the fixed stars is of the same nature as the light of the Sun, and all the systems send light into one another.

He rules all things, not as the soul of the world, but as Lord of all; and on account of his dominion he is called Lord God *pantokrator*.[^1] For *God* is a relative word and refers to servants; and *deity* is the dominion of God, not over his own body, but over servants. *The supreme God* is a Being eternal, infinite, absolutely perfect; but a being, however perfect, without dominion is not *Lord God*. For we say *my God*, *your God*, *the God of Israel*; but we do not say *my Eternal*, *your Eternal*, *the Eternal of Israel*; we do not say *my Infinite*, *your Infinite*, *the Infinite of Israel*; we do not say *my Perfect*, *your Perfect*, *the Perfect of Israel*. These titles have no relation to servants. The word *God* commonly signifies *Lord*, but not every lord is God. It is the dominion of a spiritual being that makes *God*: true dominion a true God, supreme dominion a supreme God, feigned dominion a feigned one. And from his true dominion it follows that the true God is living, intelligent and powerful; from his other perfections, that he is supreme, or supremely []{.page n=483}perfect. He is *eternal* and *infinite*, *omnipotent* and *omniscient*; that is, he endures from eternity to eternity and is present from infinity to infinity; he rules all things and knows all things that happen or can be known. He is not eternity or infinity, but eternal and infinite; he is not duration or space, but he endures and is present. He endures always and is present everywhere, and by existing always and everywhere he constitutes duration and space, eternity and infinity. Since each particle of space is *always*, and each indivisible moment of duration is *everywhere*, surely the maker and Lord of all things will not be *never* and *nowhere*. He is omnipresent not by his *power* alone, but also by his *substance*: for power cannot subsist without substance. In him[^2] all things are contained and move, but without either acting on the other. God suffers nothing from the motions of bodies; they feel no resistance from the omnipresence of God. It is agreed that the supreme God exists necessarily; and by the same necessity he is *always* and *everywhere*. Hence also he is all alike, all eye, all ear, all brain, all arm, all power of sensing, understanding and acting; but in a way not at all human, not at all bodily, a way wholly unknown to us. As a blind man has no idea of colours, so we have no idea of the ways in which the all-wise God senses and understands all things. He is wholly without any body or bodily shape, and so cannot be seen, or heard, or touched, nor ought he to be worshipped under the form of any bodily thing. We have ideas of his attributes, but what the substance of any thing is we do not know at all. We see only the shapes and colours of bodies, we hear only sounds, we touch only outer surfaces, we smell only odours, and taste flavours; inner substances we know by no sense and by no reflex act, and much less have we any idea of the substance of God. We know him only by his properties and attributes, and by the most wise and excellent structures of things, and by final causes; but we revere and worship him for his dominion. For a God without dominion, providence and final causes is nothing other than Fate and Nature. So much concerning God; to discourse of whom from the phenomena certainly belongs to *experimental philosophy*.

Thus far I have explained the phenomena of the heavens and of our sea by the force of gravity, but I have not yet assigned the cause of gravity. This force certainly arises from some cause that penetrates to the very centres of the Sun []{.page n=484}and planets without any loss of its power; and that acts not according to the quantity of the *surfaces* of the particles on which it acts (as mechanical causes do), but according to the quantity of *solid* matter; and whose action extends on all sides to immense distances, always decreasing as the squares of the distances. Gravity towards the Sun is compounded of the gravities towards the several particles of the Sun, and in receding from the Sun decreases exactly as the squares of the distances as far as the orbit of Saturn, as is plain from the rest of the planets' aphelia, and even to the farthest aphelia of the comets, provided those aphelia are at rest. But I have not yet been able to deduce the reason for these properties of gravity from the phenomena, and I do not feign hypotheses. For whatever is not deduced from the phenomena is to be called a *hypothesis*; and hypotheses, whether metaphysical or physical, of occult qualities or mechanical, have no place in *experimental philosophy*. In this philosophy propositions are deduced from the phenomena and made general by induction. Thus the impenetrability, mobility and impetus of bodies, and the laws of motion and of gravity, became known. And it is enough that gravity really exists, and acts according to the laws we have set out, and suffices for all the motions of the celestial bodies and of our sea.

We might now add something about a certain most subtle spirit that pervades gross bodies and lies hidden in them; by whose force and actions the particles of bodies attract one another at the smallest distances, and cohere when they become contiguous; and electric bodies act at greater distances, repelling as well as attracting nearby corpuscles; and light is emitted, reflected, refracted, inflected, and heats bodies; and all sensation is excited, and the limbs of animals move at will, namely by the vibrations of this spirit, propagated through the solid filaments of the nerves from the outer organs of sense to the brain, and from the brain into the muscles. But these things cannot be explained in a few words; nor is there a sufficient store of the experiments by which the laws of the actions of this spirit must be accurately determined and shown.

[^1]: That is, *universal ruler*.

[^2]: So the ancients held: *Aratus* in the *Phaenomena*, near the beginning; *Paul* in Acts 17:27, 28; *Moses* in Deuteronomy 4:39 and 10:14; *David* in Psalm 139:7, 8; *Solomon* in 1 Kings 8:27; *Job* 22:12; the prophet *Jeremiah* 23:23, 24.
