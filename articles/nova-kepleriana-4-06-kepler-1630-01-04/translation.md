# Kepler to Müller, 4 January 1630

::: {.editorial-note}
English translation of the letter as printed in *Nova Kepleriana* 4 (1927), made for Plain Old Text. Each paragraph translates the one beside it, including the German direction on the cover; Kepler's marginal note is translated as a footnote.
:::

Greetings, and omens of a most happy\
new year.

Most famous and most excellent Sir, friend of old loyalty.

Although I have had no answer from Your Excellency to this day to my two letters (for the packet meant for me has been stuck at Görlitz for 14 days now, since the man who was to see to it is away and has locked it in his chest),[^1] I am nevertheless full of hope that my letters have reached you (especially the earlier ones, handed over at Torgau by our Sagan carrier, on his way to Halberstadt, to the Leipzig messenger you know, with Angelius), and that you, with your fair-mindedness, have resolved that, just as every kind of creature, beast or man, should expect help from others of its kind, so I too, a cultivator of astronomy, should expect help from you, its public professor, above all in printing Brahe's observations. If my hope deceives me in neither, it follows that I should believe the type has already been cast and finished in the old Cicero face, and that three bales of paper have already been made by your paper-maker nearby and brought to Leipzig. So I have arranged with our merchants and the Sagan carters that, on your word, they should take whatever packs concern me from the founder and the paper-maker onto their carts and wagons. If you have already paid the founder from my money or Bartsch's, I shall hope the type will be finished as prescribed. But if the founder is willing, and it causes you no trouble, I should certainly like to inspect the goods before he receives the whole agreed price. For on your guarantee he can easily, and without risk, let the goods out of his hands, especially if he knows he has taken care and kept to the specification. But you must in no way be out of pocket: whatever you have paid out or will promise, deduct from my money and Bartsch's, without objection from us.

Of Bartsch we have only this, from the report of a Lauban student come home from Strasbourg: that he arrived at Strasbourg on 7/17 November, but on 13/23 November, the day the messenger with this news left Strasbourg, he was away from the city, gone to Durlach. What he has instructed you about his money I do not know. I do not think he will come back by way of Leipzig. So if you have no instructions to the contrary, take from what he added to my money as much as is needed. If it is otherwise, the merchant Hofkunz is ready to pay whatever more I need. My money came, if I am not mistaken, to 76 joachimstalers, of which you now hold not quite 23: to these must be added 10 for the type, or less if you can; then the three bales of paper already ordered, and two more of ordinary printing paper, which I asked Avianus to buy. The die is cast: whatever happens in public affairs, I must go on and finish what I have begun. The type ordered is absolutely needed to finish it, so that, even if I have to leave Sagan, I shall leave things whole rather than maimed and scattered. The rest I have written to M. Avianus; please share it with him. And now farewell.

Sagan, 4 January 1630.

Your Excellency's\
devoted in service,\
J. Kepler,\
Mathematician.

To the most famous and most excellent Mr\
Philipp Müller, Licentiate of Medicine,\
Professor of Astronomy in the University\
of Leipzig, my most kindly friend,\
Leipzig.

In his absence\
to be opened by Mr\
M. Wilhelm Avianus.

[^1]: [Margin:] With a receipt enclosed for 53 imperial thalers, as you asked.
