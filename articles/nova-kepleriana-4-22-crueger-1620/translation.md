# Crüger to Müller, Easter Monday 1620

::: {.editorial-note}
English translation of the Latin extracts printed in *Nova Kepleriana* 4 (1927), made for Plain Old Text. Each paragraph translates the extract beside it; the omission marks (…) are the 1927 editors'. Italics follow the editors' emphasis.
:::

Kepler's books arrived in good time, but I am still waiting for the title page of the *Comet* that you promised me. I am greatly and dutifully obliged to you.

This Author's books on comets are written most ingeniously to establish that comets move in straight lines. Even so, I cannot subscribe to this motion of comets, and I have two reasons. The first is that before anything else he assumes the earth's annual motion as clearly proved, when the case is still before the judge. Though I believe in the earth's daily motion, I cannot bring myself to accept the annual one. …

The other reason that keeps me from embracing the straight-line motion of comets is that a straight path can in no way represent a comet's own motion where it grows beyond a semicircle. …

The theorems of Kepler cited above suppose a marvellous motion of the comet, never dreamt of or heard of in any comet. Theorem XXI, obscure enough, he illustrated with no diagram; but I have overcome its obscurity, and I send you a diagram of it here, agreeing with every word of his. Weigh it carefully, I beg you, and see the comet's rabid leaps one moment and idle standstills the next. Who (confound it!) since the world was made ever saw or heard of such a thing? Kepler seems, then, to sing the triumph before the victory. … But I shall stop here, fearing to offend you, who think so highly of Kepler, with this criticism. Whatever I have done, I have done as a student of astronomical truth, which I believe is a friend to you above all your friends.

… *His work on Mars* demands a man free of every other thought, and that not for one day but for a whole year, until it can be grasped throughout. I would dare to say that the man has worked most zealously at being obscure and intricate …

*Kepler's Harmony of the World* I have not yet read through attentively. This work seems as obscure as the one on Mars …
