[]{.page .unprinted n=1}A NEW\
SOLID GEOMETRY\
OF WINE BARRELS,\
above all of the Austrian barrel, the most fitting shape of all;

AND

THE USE IN IT OF THE CUBIC\
gauging rod, most convenient and quite unique.

To which is added\
A SUPPLEMENT\
TO THE SOLID GEOMETRY OF ARCHIMEDES.

By the Author\
Johannes Kepler, Mathematician to the Emperor Matthias I\
and to his faithful Estates of Austria above the Enns.

*With an Imperial privilege for 15 years.*

*IN THE YEAR 1615.*

AT LINZ

Printed by JOHANNES PLANCK, at the Author's expense.

## []{.page .unprinted n=3}To the Most Illustrious Lord

LORD MAXIMILIAN,\
Lord of Liechtenstein and Nikolsburg, Lord of Rabensburg, Hohenau, Butschowitz, Poscheritz and Neograd,\
Councillor of His Holy Imperial Majesty,\
Chamberlain and Master of the Horse, etc.

And also\
to the Illustrious and Noble Lord\
LORD HELMHARD JÖRGER,\
of Tollet, Keppach, Grebing and Hernals, Lord of Steyregg and Erlach, Free Baron of Kreuzbach, Hereditary Steward of the Court of the Province of the Archduchy of Austria above the Enns:\
Councillor of His Holy Imperial Majesty in the Court Chamber; and at present one of the Barons in the Estates of the said Province;

*My most gracious Lords*

Last November, Most Illustrious Lord, Illustrious and Noble Lord Baron, my most gracious Lords, I had brought a new bride home. It was a time when Austria, having gathered a vintage both plentiful and noble, was sending a great many cargo boats up the Danube and sharing out her wealth with our Noricum, and the whole bank at Linz could be seen blocked with wine casks on sale at a bearable price. It was fitting to the duty of a husband and a good father of a family that I should provide my household with drink. So when a few casks had been brought home and stored, the seller came four days later with a gauging rod, and with one and the same rod he measured all the casks alike, without distinction, without regard to their shape, without reasoning or calculation. He let the brass point of the rod down through the filling-hole of the full cask, slantwise to the edge of each of the two wooden discs that in everyday speech we call the heads; and when this length, from the top of the belly to the bottom of each round board, appeared equal on both sides, he read off the number of measures the cask held from the mark of the number stamped on the rod at the place where the length ended; and the price was reckoned by that number.

I was astonished that a line drawn slantwise through the body of half a cask could be any evidence of its capacity. I doubted too the reliability of this measurement, since a cask very short between its two discs, with discs only a little wider, and therefore of very small capacity, might have the same length from the filling-hole to the bottom of either disc. There came to mind the laborious measurement used on the Rhine: there they either fill the casks and go through them counting every measure of liquid, at a tedious cost of time, and brand the casks they have tested with marks of their capacity; or, if they use gauging rods, they mostly measure the diameters of the discs and the length of the curved staves, multiply these together, and apply various corrections for the inequality of the discs, the width of the belly and the curvature of the staves. Nor do they agree with one another; rather, each accuses the others of error.

Since, then, I had learned that this use of the slanting rod had been established here by public authority, and that the gaugers were sworn to it, it seemed not unfitting for a new husband to take as a new beginning of mathematical labours the testing, by the laws of geometry, of the certainty of this convenient measurement, so necessary to the household, and to bring its foundations to light, if it had any.

When this inquiry had gone forward over the last three days with a pleasant variety, so that something certain could be declared, and I was taking up my pen to write out and polish this demonstration, since it was already grasped in my mind, I did not have to search long for those whom I should address in a dedication, which was the beginning of the little book: men whose uprightness of mind would match the exactness of demonstrations, and who would pursue their beauty with a singular eagerness. For such a man your physician, Dr John Wedderburn the Scot, a man most practised in the mathematical arts and for that reason a very close friend of mine, described you to me, Most Illustrious Lord of Liechtenstein; he happened to arrive at the right moment and by his presence renewed my memory of you. Such a man, too, I had long known you to be from long acquaintance, Illustrious and Noble Lord Baron Jörger; and in this merit you are so equal that I might seem to do one of you an injury, being the client of both, if I named the other alone.

And what should prevent me from naming you colleagues here? For in this business there is no rivalry of nobility, of rank, of political virtues, or of anything a mathematician is wont to look at, but only of intellect and, if I may add it, of being my patron.

Without any hesitation, therefore, I resolved to put together from my inquiry a New Year's gift for Your Lordships for the coming Kalends of January, by which we might on both sides be reminded of our gratitude to God for these and the other benefits received in the past year; and by which at the same time you, as patrons, might take pleasure from the dedication of a welcome thing, and I, as author, from intelligent readers and judges, each in the other; and so that this glory of our Austria, the method of measuring wine, might have for its patrons the chief men of the Austrian nobility above all, owners of the widest vineyards, who have wine enough even for generosity.

Farewell, Noble Lords; delight your minds with a most beautiful inquiry, your accustomed pleasure, pass the coming year cheerful and blessed with every kind of good, and continue, as you are accustomed, to favour me with your grace. Linz, the 16th day before the Kalends of January, in the year of the Western Christians 1613.

Your Most Illustrious and Illustrious Lordships'\
*Most devoted*\
Mathematician to the Emperor Matthias\
and to his faithful Estates of Austria above the Enns,\
Johannes Kepler.
