# Title Page and Letters to Philipp Müller

English translation of the 1630 printing, made for Plain Old Text. Each paragraph translates the corresponding paragraph of the original.

[]{.page .unprinted n=1}ADMONITION\
OF JOHANNES KEPLER,\
IMPERIAL MATHEMATICIAN

TO ASTRONOMERS,\
AND STUDENTS\
OF HEAVENLY THINGS,

On the rare and wonderful Phenomena\
of the Year 1631,

*NAMELY THE INCURSION OF VENUS AND MERCURY*\
*into the Sun:*

*Excerpted*\
From the Ephemeris for the Year 1631, placed before it on the Author's express advice, and published again

*by*\
*JACOB BARTSCH*\
*of Lauban in Lusatia, Doctor of Philosophy and Medicine.*

At Frankfurt\
By Godefrid Tampach.\
In the Year 1630.

## []{.page n=3}Most famous and most excellent Sir, Lord Philipp Müller

LICENTIATE OF MEDICINE AND PROFESSOR OF MATHEMATICS IN THE UNIVERSITY OF LEIPZIG,\
most renowned public professor,\
*Patron and Friend to be honoured for ever.*

The writing of Kepler's Admonition, first printed at Leipzig toward the end of last year, at the start of my recent journey to Strasbourg, on the Author's express advice and for the Reader's pleasant use, and inscribed to your most honoured name, I presented to you in your house, to your great pleasure. Now, at the wished-for happy end of the same journey, while I am forced to stay at Frankfurt on my way home, at the beginning of this year, the same writing has been reprinted with the Author's consent, on a new arrangement of the bookseller's; and, inscribed once more to your most famous name, I do not hesitate, for the sake of good omen, to send it ahead to Leipzig, to your Muses, who hold it most dear. To the reasons given in the earlier little preface, now repeated for this purpose, I add this new one: that after the bond of closer kinship with the oft-praised Lord Kepler has now been confirmed, and the honour of the medical degree added, I credit to you once more this same writing, whether first or second, mine and not mine. Take this meanwhile in good part, until I offer our Ephemerides, now in the press in Silesia and soon (as you know besides) to appear; and do not cease to favour your Bartsch. Frankfurt, from the spring fair, in the year 1630, just at the meeting of the Sun and Mercury opposite the Moon, by the new Tychonic-Keplerian reckoning.

*Your Excellency's,*\
*honouring you for ever,*\
Jac. Bartsch, D.

## []{.page n=4}First dedication

*To the most excellent Mathematician of Leipzig, the most distinguished Lord*\
PHILIPP MÜLLER,\
LICENTIATE OF MEDICINE AND PUBLIC PROFESSOR, my Lord PATRON,\
formerly my Teacher, now my Host,\
most honoured.

*GREETING AND SAFETY in every way.*

While I was on my journey, Sir and Patron to be honoured for ever, and that stepmother chance compelled me to stay at Leipzig, both when leaving here lately and now when passing through again, another and kinder chance willed that I should live with you, discuss pleasant astronomical matters, and take counsel for the common good. For it came about, as you know, that there was leisure to talk at greater length face to face, not only about the plans and present studies of the famous Lord Kepler, whom I rightly revere as our common Master in a parent's place, and about the almost threefold reason for my journey, bound once more for Frankfurt and Strasbourg: but above all the new and more correct logarithmic calculus of the Rudolphine astronomical tables, and the first volume, now born of it, of our twenty-year Ephemerides, while it already awaits the press and the press hardly hurries, seemed to demand some sure foretaste of them, with both fairs waiting.

Therefore, to make up in some measure for this delay while I stay at Leipzig, and to satisfy meanwhile the astrophile eager for Keplerian matters; and, at your urging too, and without the Author's dissent, to give those far away advance notice of a new and wonderful celestial event: I did not hesitate to pluck from the body of our Ephemerides a not contemptible morsel, and, set out in public type, []{.page n=5}to consecrate it and offer it as owed to you, as foremost of astronomers, and as one who most illuminates the academic pole with your splendour.

For you who were *first* my Doctor of the Art in the University nine years ago, and who supplied the *first* foundations for my *first* astronomical book, the Starry Planisphere published at Strasbourg five years ago; who also *first*, when I came home to my country two years ago, as guide again to the Muses of Lord Kepler, gave me, by a kindly fate a year ago, the *first* news of his fortunate and auspicious arrival in the regions near my country: you also rightly appear now as the *first* (to keep silent on other, private reasons) to whom, after this year's Ephemeris of mine, published here under your auspices too, I publicly credit this *first* writing, mine and not mine, and the vow of closer kinship with the oft-praised Lord Kepler himself.

Here then, most distinguished Sir, is the not unwelcome paper token of a grateful Pupil, the public token of a grateful heart, and finally of a grateful Guest, by which (since you refuse any other) I discharge for now not only my debt to you, but also Lord Kepler's debt to astronomers: take it now in good part, and do not cease to favour me and my studies, as you abundantly do. Written in your house. Leipzig, 1629, 5/15 October.

Your Excellency's, dutifully honouring you,\
J. Bartsch, Ph. M. & C.P.
