NOVA KEPLERIANA 4: WHAT A HUMAN STILL NEEDS TO CHECK
=====================================================

The English translations of all 34 texts in Nova Kepleriana 4
(https://sources.vjbe.net/stable/713cz), their editor's notes, and the
side-by-side translation view were all written by Claude (October 2026) and
have not been reviewed by a person. This file lists what most needs checking.
Each translation is articles/<slug>/translation.md; its notes are the entries
with "edition": "translation" in that folder's data.json.

Tick items off, or delete them, as they are done.


1. GENERAL
----------

[ ] Read every translation against the Latin (and German) once. Nothing has
    been checked by a Latinist.
[ ] Each English paragraph must stay matched to its Latin paragraph: the page
    pairs them by position. scripts/check-notes.php does not check this. After
    any edit, open /stable/<id>?translation=1 and make sure the rows still line
    up.
[ ] Conventions to confirm or change:
    - Words the 1927 editors restored from the copies (shown in [brackets] in
      the Latin) are written out without brackets in the English.
    - Folio marks such as (S. 92b) are left out of the English.
    - [sign omitted], [cut out], [illegible], [gap], [!] and […] are kept.
    - Delisle's French shelf labels (introduction) and Kepler's Latin title for
      the fair catalogue (26 Aug 1630) are left untranslated.
    - "Pernegger" is given as Bernegger in the English, with a note.
    - Kepler's "concinnus" is "melodic" and "consonus" "consonant" (postscript
      on Bartolus); his "sesquadrus" is rendered with the coinage "sesquadrate".

3. UNCERTAIN READINGS, TEXT BY TEXT
-----------------------------------

Kepler to Seussius, 15 July 1622 (/stable/kk1cg), the hardest text
[ ] "teque vicissim remuneror me, sed opus Argentina me petas a Perneggero
    professore, sane quoque capitosum": read as a joke about an engraved
    portrait of Kepler with too big a head, to be had from Bernegger at
    Strasbourg, with Lansius's epigram beneath. Check, and check the note.
[ ] "Omnia plena malis, FASTIque, audaxque CATHEDRA": translated literally.
    What the capitals point at (titles? a chronogram?) is unknown.
[ ] "ille Faulhaberi similis insecti, usitatae infoelicitatis Antagonista":
    rendered "an insect of the same sort as Faulhaber, an adversary of the
    usual misfortune". The sense is unclear.
[ ] "Kanones pueriles": translated as a further anagram of Ioannes Keplerus
    added as the title. The letters do match; check that this is the sense.
[ ] "Thesi XXV … imaginem Crucis a me in Cygnum inductam" and the Tycho and
    Cassiopeia sentence: translated without notes. They may deserve some.
[ ] "Ambrosius Rhodius … in Ara et Thuribulo": no note. The reference is
    unexplained.

Kepler's postscript on Bartolus, 1622 (/stable/2wbpc)
[ ] "pene summa et penima": rendered "voices near the top and near the
    bottom".
[ ] "Dassius": left as a name, unidentified.
[ ] "stant post principia": rendered "stand in the second line", with a note on
    the Roman battle order.

Kepler to Müller, 17/27 October 1629 (/stable/pqxke)
[ ] "Martis quippe alumnus et tantum non pullus": "a nursling of Mars, indeed
    all but his chick".
[ ] The paragraph on the "Danish calculation" (epicycle of Venus and Mercury,
    equant point, secular equation) is technical. Check it against the
    astronomy.

Kepler to Müller, 13/23 November 1629 (/stable/j9ged)
[ ] "neque illius ad Perneggerum, quibus de sua testaretur constantia, ad me
    quoque fortasse non pervenissent": read as "nor would her letters … have
    reached Bernegger, or perhaps me either".
[ ] "receptis intercessoriis Principis, necdum resignatis": "not yet unsealed"
    (could be "not yet answered").
[ ] "differri inter praesens placitum, et incognitum necessarium" (footnote 3):
    "between a present liking and an unknown duty".
[ ] Footnote 2 ("Missis ad manus filiae meae dehortatoriis…"): who "he" is
    (the suitor or Bernegger) is left ambiguous, as in the Latin.
[ ] The German postscript and cover: "Kegel" is rendered "body" (of type),
    "Garemond-Kegel" "Garamond body"; "Mr Avianus" is not identified.

Kepler to Müller, 4 January 1630 (/stable/6nfh1)
[ ] "In abweßen Herrn M. Wilhelm Aviano zu erbrechen": "In his absence to be
    opened by Mr M. Wilhelm Avianus".

Kepler to Müller, 16/26 January 1630 (/stable/68txp)
[ ] "fide ea quam de te habent Grossiani, satagis": "you are hard pressed by
    the trust the Grosses place in you". Who "Grossiani" are is not noted;
    probably the Leipzig booksellers of the Grosse family.
[ ] "si quatuor impressas in forma majori conjunctum sunt": garbled Latin,
    rendered loosely.
[ ] "jam enim alter illi currenti mensis ad finem venit": "the other half is
    due to reach him at the end of this month".

Kepler to Müller, 27 February 1630 (/stable/yv4q1)
[ ] "Gratulor dolens de dolentibus, Germanus de alto Regum, nunc quidem etiam
    Germano, sanguine": translated literally; the sense is unclear.
[ ] "Eo qua licet; foelicior. non sanctior, qui qua libet": "I go where I may;
    happier, not holier, is he who goes where he pleases".

Kepler to Müller, 22 April 1630 (/stable/sj54x)
[ ] "nec omnes suis rhabdis commode aptatae": "their rods". The meaning of
    rhabdi in type-founding is uncertain.
[ ] "quas non [sign omitted] sed demum exsculpsit primum ex chalybe": unclear.
[ ] "dum [sign omitted] annus est": left as is.

Kepler to Müller, 26 August 1630 (/stable/zq6m1)
[ ] "pro pretio stato et ab aliis penso": "at the fixed price that others pay".

Kepler to Müller, 2 September 1630 (/stable/kve91)
[ ] "manente nobis titulo, restituta suis dominis provincia": "with the title
    left to us and the province restored to its lords".
[ ] "nunciosque rerum laetarum, si Lipsiae vili pretio prostant": read as a
    joke ("if news of happy events is going cheap at Leipzig, send me some").

Bartsch's open letter, 1628 (/stable/cbvzd)
[ ] "Astro-poecilo-pyrgium" is left untranslated.
[ ] The closing "qui plurimum et salvere et valere Te jubet / Jac. Bartschio
    Authori" is read as the object of "favere" ("go on favouring … the one who
    wishes you health … Jac. Bartsch, the Author").
[ ] The long sentence on the "practical spheres" (instrumental equatoria) is
    technical. Check the astronomical terms.

Bartsch to Müller, 9/19 January 1630 (/stable/cha7k)
[ ] "dominus parens" is read as Kepler (Bartsch's father-in-law to be), not
    Bartsch's own father, with a note saying Bartsch's father had died in 1628.
    Check both.
[ ] "Princeps" is read as the Margrave of Baden; the note says Susanna had been
    living at his court at Durlach. Check.

Bartsch to Müller, 5 May 1631 (/stable/h4gas)
[ ] "Dn. compater": "my son's godfather".
[ ] "ex abrupto, me sine periculi metu me … expulerunt": rendered "suddenly,
    when I feared no danger". The Latin may be corrupt (perhaps "non sine").
[ ] "profiliolo": first translated "little son", corrected to "your little
    godson" after Müller's letter of 8 May. Check this reading.
[ ] "multos enim praeter opinionem ad ordinem sumtus": "far more than I
    expected"; "ad ordinem" is not rendered.

Müller to Bartsch, 8 May 1631 (/stable/fmr23)
[ ] "gamelii": "the wedding poem". It is not known which poem is meant.
[ ] "constituto a R. precio": left as "the price fixed by R.".
[ ] "Domine conpater": "father of my godson".

Bartsch to Müller, 19/29 June 1631 (/stable/6476h)
[ ] The sentence on the logarithms ("Logarithmos indicis meos exactissimos
    comprobat…") is rendered loosely.
[ ] "Jam a me et mea, nostroque": "from me, my wife and our boy".

Crüger to Müller, Easter Monday 1620 (/stable/c2nge)
[ ] "titulum Cometae": "the title page of the Comet".
[ ] "Theoremata Keppleri citata": "the theorems of Kepler cited above". The
    extract doesn't show who cited them.

Crüger to Müller, 1 July 1622 (/stable/chzxw)
[ ] "Kepplerus ipsas observationes limitet ad libros suos Harmonicos…": "Kepler
    trims the observations themselves to fit his books on harmony…".
